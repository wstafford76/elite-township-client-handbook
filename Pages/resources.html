Real Estate Glossary

Real estate transactions can involve unfamiliar terms and abbreviations. This glossary provides a general explanation of commonly used terms.

Appraisal

An opinion of a property's value prepared by a qualified appraiser. Lenders may require an appraisal as part of a financed transaction.

Closing

The process of completing the documents and requirements necessary to finalize a real estate transaction.

Closing Costs

Expenses associated with completing a real estate transaction. Closing costs can vary depending on the transaction, property, financing, and other circumstances.

Comparative Market Analysis (CMA)

An analysis prepared by a real estate professional using market data and comparable properties to help estimate a property's potential market value or pricing position. A CMA is not an appraisal.

Earnest Money

Money provided according to a purchase contract to demonstrate a buyer's commitment to the transaction. The handling and disposition of earnest money are governed by the contract and applicable rules.

Equity

Generally, the difference between a property's value and the amount owed against it, subject to the circumstances and obligations affecting the property.

Inspection

An evaluation of a property's condition by a qualified inspector or other professional. An inspection can help identify conditions or concerns that may require further evaluation.

Listing Agreement

An agreement between a property owner and a real estate broker that establishes the terms of the brokerage relationship for marketing and potentially selling the property.

MLS

Multiple Listing Service. A system used by participating real estate professionals to share property listing information and cooperate with other participants.

Mortgage

A loan used to finance real property, generally secured by the property.

Pre-Approval

A lender's preliminary evaluation of a potential borrower's financial information and borrowing capacity. Requirements and the meaning of a pre-approval can vary by lender.

Title

The legal rights or ownership interest associated with a property. A title company may assist with examining title and handling parts of the closing process.

Title Insurance

Insurance designed to protect against certain covered title issues, subject to the terms, conditions, and exclusions of the policy.

Important Contacts & Information to Keep

Keeping important contact information and documents organized can make your real estate or rental experience easier to manage.

Real Estate & Property Contacts

  • Your real estate agent or broker
  • Lender or mortgage representative, when applicable
  • Title company or closing contact, when applicable
  • Property manager or landlord, when applicable
  • Home inspector or other service professionals, when applicable

Property Information

  • Property address
  • Important deadlines and dates
  • Utility provider information
  • HOA or community contact information, when applicable
  • Maintenance or emergency contact instructions, when applicable

Important Documents

  • Signed contracts, leases, and addenda
  • Inspection reports or property-condition documentation
  • Closing documents, when applicable
  • Payment confirmations and receipts
  • Important written communications
  • Property or move-in/move-out documentation

Keep Your Records Organized

Consider keeping important documents and communications in one secure location where you can easily find them. Maintain copies of documents that may be needed later for reference, questions, or recordkeeping.

Quick Tip: Create a dedicated digital folder for your real estate or rental documents and save important emails, signed documents, receipts, and property information in one place.

Payment & Wire Fraud Protection

Real estate and rental transactions can involve significant payments and sensitive financial information. Criminals may attempt to impersonate a real estate professional, title company, landlord, property manager, or other party in order to redirect payments or obtain personal information.

Be Careful With Changed Payment Instructions

Treat unexpected changes to payment or wiring instructions as a potential warning sign. Before sending money, independently verify the instructions using a trusted phone number or contact method you already know.

Common Warning Signs

  • An unexpected request to change where money should be sent.
  • Pressure to send money immediately.
  • An email address or message that appears slightly different from a known contact.
  • Requests to communicate or pay through an unfamiliar method.
  • Instructions that conflict with information you previously received.

Before Sending Money

  • Verify payment instructions independently.
  • Use a trusted phone number rather than one included in a suspicious message.
  • Confirm the recipient and payment details carefully.
  • Do not rely solely on an unexpected email, text message, or attachment.
  • Ask questions if anything appears unusual or inconsistent.

If You Suspect Fraud

Stop before sending additional money or information. Contact the legitimate company or individual through a trusted method and report the situation to the appropriate financial institution or other relevant authority when necessary.

Safety Tip: Never assume that payment or wiring instructions are legitimate simply because they appear to come from someone involved in your transaction. Verify important payment instructions independently.

Moving & Address Change Checklist

Moving involves more than packing and transporting belongings. Use this checklist as a general reminder of tasks you may need to complete before and after changing residences.

Before Your Move

  • Confirm your move-out and move-in dates.
  • Schedule movers or arrange transportation if needed.
  • Review utility transfer, activation, or cancellation requirements.
  • Update service providers when appropriate.
  • Review any property-specific moving instructions.
  • Organize important documents and valuables.

Update Your Address

  • Arrange mail forwarding when appropriate.
  • Update your employer or payroll information.
  • Update banks, financial institutions, and insurance providers.
  • Update subscriptions, memberships, and important accounts.
  • Update your driver's license or other records when required.
  • Notify schools, healthcare providers, or other important contacts as needed.

At Your New Property

  • Document the property's condition when appropriate.
  • Confirm utilities and essential services are working.
  • Locate important safety features and shutoffs when appropriate.
  • Keep important property documents and contact information organized.
  • Update emergency contact information if necessary.
Quick Tip: Create a moving checklist early and update it as you receive new instructions or identify additional tasks specific to your move.

Emergency & Property Safety Information

Knowing what to do during an emergency or property-related safety issue can help you respond more quickly and communicate important information to the appropriate people.

Keep Important Emergency Contacts Available

  • Emergency services
  • Your property manager or landlord, when applicable
  • Your HOA or community emergency contact, when applicable
  • Your utility providers
  • Your insurance company or insurance representative
  • Other property-specific emergency contacts

Know Your Property's Important Safety Features

When appropriate, familiarize yourself with the location and operation of important safety features and systems in the property.

  • Smoke alarms
  • Carbon monoxide alarms, when applicable
  • Fire extinguishers, when available
  • Main water shutoff
  • Electrical panel
  • Gas shutoff information, when applicable

Water Leaks & Property Damage

If you discover an active water leak or another issue that could cause significant property damage, take reasonable steps to protect your safety and limit further damage when it is safe to do so. Contact the appropriate property representative, service provider, or emergency service based on the situation.

Fire, Gas, or Immediate Safety Emergencies

If there is an immediate threat to life or safety, leave the area when necessary and contact emergency services. Do not place yourself at risk attempting to investigate or correct a dangerous situation.

After an Emergency

  • Document property damage when it is safe to do so.
  • Keep records of important communications and instructions.
  • Contact your insurance provider when appropriate.
  • Follow instructions provided by emergency personnel or the appropriate property representative.
Safety Tip: Before you need it, take a few minutes to identify important emergency contacts and familiarize yourself with the major safety features of your property.

Utilities & Home Services Guide

Utility and home service responsibilities can vary depending on the property, your lease, purchase agreement, closing arrangements, or other applicable agreements. Confirm which services are your responsibility and when accounts need to be started, transferred, changed, or canceled.

Common Utility & Home Services

  • Electricity
  • Water and wastewater services
  • Natural gas or propane, when applicable
  • Trash and recycling services
  • Internet and television services
  • Security or monitoring services, when applicable

Before Starting or Transferring Service

  • Confirm which services are your responsibility.
  • Determine the date service should begin or transfer.
  • Verify the service address carefully.
  • Ask whether a deposit, connection fee, or other charge applies.
  • Keep confirmation numbers and account information.

When Moving Out or Completing a Transaction

  • Confirm the date services should end or transfer.
  • Provide any required forwarding or final billing information.
  • Keep confirmation of service cancellation or transfer.
  • Review final bills and payment records.
  • Confirm that any services remaining with the property are handled appropriately.

Who Is Responsible?

Do not assume that a particular utility or service is included or automatically transferred. Review your lease, contract, closing instructions, or other applicable agreement and confirm responsibilities with the appropriate party when necessary.

Keep Your Information Organized

Maintain a record of provider names, account numbers, confirmation numbers, service dates, and important contact information. This can make it easier to resolve billing questions or service issues later.

Quick Tip: Schedule utilities and essential services early enough to help ensure they are active when you need them, especially when moving into a new property.

Home Maintenance & Seasonal Planning

Regular attention to a property's condition can help identify maintenance needs before they become larger problems. Responsibility for maintenance and repairs can vary depending on ownership, lease terms, property rules, and other applicable agreements.

Regular Property Checks

  • Watch for signs of water leaks or moisture.
  • Monitor heating and cooling performance.
  • Check visible doors, windows, and exterior areas for changes in condition.
  • Pay attention to unusual sounds, odors, or changes in utility usage.
  • Address maintenance concerns through the appropriate process.

Seasonal Planning

  • Prepare for severe weather when appropriate.
  • Review outdoor areas and drainage before periods of heavy rain.
  • Monitor heating or cooling systems before periods of heavy use.
  • Keep exterior areas reasonably clear and maintained when responsible.
  • Review property-specific maintenance needs throughout the year.

When You Are Not Responsible for Repairs

If you rent the property or another party is responsible for a particular repair, follow the required reporting process rather than assuming you should perform the repair yourself. Review your lease, agreement, or property instructions when questions arise.

Keep Maintenance Records

Keep records of important maintenance requests, repairs, invoices, warranties, and communications when applicable. Organized records can be useful when tracking recurring issues or reviewing work that has been completed.

Quick Tip: Address small issues early when appropriate. A minor leak, unusual sound, or change in how a system operates can sometimes indicate a problem that may become more significant over time.

Property Insurance Basics

Insurance needs can vary depending on whether you own, rent, or are preparing to purchase a property. Policies, coverage, deductibles, exclusions, and requirements can vary significantly, so review your specific policy and speak with a qualified insurance professional when you have questions about coverage.

Homeowners Insurance

Homeowners insurance is generally associated with owner-occupied property and may provide coverage for certain property damage, liability, or other covered losses, subject to the terms and conditions of the policy.

Renters Insurance

Renters insurance is generally designed to help protect a tenant's personal property and may provide liability or other coverage, depending on the policy. A landlord's insurance policy may not protect a tenant's personal belongings.

Flood and Other Separate Coverage

Some types of losses may not be covered by a standard property insurance policy. Depending on the property, location, and policy, separate or additional coverage may be available or required.

Before Closing or Moving In

  • Determine whether insurance coverage is required.
  • Confirm when coverage must become effective.
  • Review deductibles, coverage limits, and important exclusions.
  • Keep copies of policy and agent contact information.
  • Provide proof of insurance when required.

After a Property Loss

When safe to do so, document the condition of the property and follow the reporting procedures provided by your insurance company. Take reasonable steps to prevent additional damage when appropriate and safe, and keep records of important communications and expenses.

Quick Tip: Review your insurance coverage before you need it. Understanding your policy, deductible, and important exclusions can help you know what information to provide if a loss occurs.

Property Taxes & Homestead Basics

Property taxes and available exemptions can affect the cost of owning real estate. Tax assessments, exemptions, deadlines, and eligibility requirements can vary based on the property, owner, and applicable taxing authorities.

Understanding Property Taxes

Property taxes are generally based on the taxable value of a property and applicable tax rates. Different taxing entities may contribute to the total property tax bill.

Appraised Value and Market Value

A value used for property tax purposes may differ from a property's current market value or a price that could be obtained in a real estate transaction. Property tax values are determined through the applicable appraisal process and should not automatically be treated as a current market valuation.

Homestead Exemptions

Certain property owners may qualify for homestead or other exemptions that can reduce the taxable value of a qualifying property. Eligibility, application requirements, deadlines, and available exemptions can vary.

After Purchasing a Property

  • Review the property's current tax information.
  • Determine whether you may qualify for an exemption.
  • Review applicable filing requirements and deadlines.
  • Keep copies of important tax and exemption records.
  • Contact the appropriate appraisal district or tax professional when you have questions.

Why Property Taxes Can Change

Property taxes may change due to factors such as changes in property value, tax rates, exemptions, ownership, improvements, or other circumstances affecting the property.

Quick Tip: Do not assume that a property's previous tax bill will remain the same after a purchase. Review current information and verify any available exemptions or filing requirements with the appropriate authority.
