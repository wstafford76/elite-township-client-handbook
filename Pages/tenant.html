Step 7: Maintenance, Repairs & Property Issues

Maintenance issues can happen during any tenancy. Reporting problems promptly and following the property's maintenance procedures can help prevent small issues from becoming larger ones.

When Something Needs Attention

✓
Report maintenance issues using the method required by your lease.
✓
Provide clear information about the problem.
✓
Include photographs or videos when appropriate.
✓
Report leaks, water intrusion, electrical problems, or other serious concerns promptly.
✓
Follow instructions provided by the landlord or property manager.

Emergency Situations

If there is an immediate threat to life, safety, or the property, follow the appropriate emergency procedures and contact emergency services when necessary. For property emergencies, also follow the emergency maintenance instructions provided by your landlord or property manager.

Do Not Make Unauthorized Changes

Do not make significant repairs, alterations, installations, or modifications to the property unless they are permitted by your lease or authorized by the appropriate party.

Document Your Requests

Keep records of maintenance requests and important communications related to property issues. Documentation can help you track when a concern was reported and what actions were taken.

Agent Tip: If you're unsure whether an issue is your responsibility or the landlord's responsibility, check your lease and contact the landlord or property manager before taking action.

Rental Scams & Fraud Protection

Rental scams can involve fake listings, individuals pretending to represent a property owner, or requests for money before a legitimate rental transaction has been confirmed. Taking time to verify the property and the person you are dealing with can help reduce your risk.

Watch for Warning Signs

✓
The advertised rent appears unusually low compared with similar properties.
✓
You are pressured to send money immediately.
✓
You are asked to send money before verifying the property or transaction.
✓
The person refuses to provide clear information about the property.
✓
The listing information appears inconsistent or copied from another source.
✓
You are asked to communicate or pay through an unusual or unverified method.

Verify Before You Pay

✓
Confirm that the property is actually available for rent.
✓
Verify the identity and authority of the person or company representing the property.
✓
Review the rental terms and required payments carefully.
✓
Confirm where payments should be sent before transferring funds.
✓
Keep records of important communications and payment confirmations.

Protect Your Personal Information

Rental applications can require sensitive personal and financial information. Before providing documents or information, make sure you understand who is requesting it and why it is needed. Use caution when sharing sensitive information through unsecured or unfamiliar methods.

If Something Doesn't Seem Right

Pause before sending money or sensitive information. Ask questions, independently verify the information you have received, and avoid making rushed decisions based solely on pressure or urgency.

Safety Tip: If you suspect a rental listing or payment request may be fraudulent, do not send additional money or sensitive information until you have verified the situation.

Tenant Frequently Asked Questions

How much money will I need before moving in?

The amount due before move-in can vary by property and may include rent, a security deposit, application fees, pet-related charges, or other amounts permitted by the lease or rental agreement. Confirm all required amounts and payment instructions before move-in.

Who is responsible for utilities?

Utility responsibilities depend on the property and lease. Review your lease and confirm which utilities or services must be placed in your name.

Can I make changes to the property?

Your lease may restrict alterations, installations, painting, or other changes to the property. Review the lease and obtain any required authorization before making changes.

What should I do if something needs repair?

Follow the maintenance reporting procedure provided by the landlord or property manager. Report issues promptly and keep records of important requests and communications.

What happens if I pay rent late?

Late-payment consequences can depend on the lease and applicable law. Review your lease for due dates, fees, notice requirements, and other provisions related to late payments.

Can guests stay at the property?

Guest and occupancy rules may be addressed in the lease or property rules. Review those requirements if a guest will be staying for an extended period.

What should I do if I want to move out?

Review your lease and move-out requirements, including any required notice. Follow the instructions provided by the landlord or property manager and keep copies of important communications.

Can I end my lease early?

Early termination may involve specific procedures, financial obligations, or other consequences. Review your lease and contact the landlord or property manager before making plans to leave early.

What should I do if I receive a notice from my landlord?

Read the notice carefully, review the applicable lease provisions, pay attention to deadlines, and keep a copy of the notice. Seek clarification or appropriate professional guidance when necessary.

Important: This guide provides general information and does not replace your lease or legal advice. Your specific rights and responsibilities may depend on your lease, the circumstances involved, and applicable law.
