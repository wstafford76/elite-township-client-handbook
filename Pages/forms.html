Understanding Real Estate Forms & Documents

Real estate and rental transactions can involve multiple forms, agreements, disclosures, notices, and other documents. The documents involved in your situation will depend on the type of transaction, property, parties involved, and other circumstances.

Why Documents Are Important

Forms and documents may establish responsibilities, deadlines, permissions, disclosures, financial obligations, or other important terms. Take time to review documents carefully before signing.

Common Types of Documents

  • Representation or service agreements
  • Purchase or lease agreements
  • Disclosures and notices
  • Addenda and amendments
  • Property-condition documentation
  • Closing or move-in/move-out documents

Before You Sign

  • Review the document carefully.
  • Confirm that important names, dates, and property information are correct.
  • Ask questions about terms or sections you do not understand.
  • Understand any deadlines or obligations created by the document.
  • Keep a copy of signed documents for your records.
Important: Your real estate professional can help explain the purpose of documents involved in your transaction, but certain questions may require guidance from an attorney, lender, tax professional, inspector, insurance professional, or another qualified specialist.

Buyer Forms & Documents

Buyers may encounter several types of documents before, during, and after purchasing a property. The specific forms used will depend on the transaction and the circumstances involved.

Representation & Service Agreements

A buyer may be asked to review an agreement describing the relationship between the buyer and a real estate broker or agent, including the services to be provided and other important terms.

Property Search & Financial Documents

  • Pre-approval or other lender documentation
  • Property information and listing details
  • Required notices or disclosures
  • Other documents requested during the transaction

Purchase Contract & Addenda

Once a buyer decides to make an offer, the transaction may involve a purchase contract and additional documents or addenda. These documents can address matters such as financing, property conditions, deadlines, included items, and other transaction-specific terms.

Property & Inspection Documents

  • Seller disclosures or other required property information
  • Inspection reports
  • Repair-related agreements or amendments, when applicable
  • HOA or community documents, when applicable

Closing Documents

Before and at closing, buyers may receive documents from the lender, title company, and other parties involved in the transaction. Review important documents carefully and keep copies for your records.

Quick Tip: Create a dedicated folder for your purchase documents and organize them by stage of the transaction. This can make deadlines, requests, and important information easier to track.

Seller Forms & Documents

Sellers may encounter several documents throughout the listing and sale of a property. The specific forms used will depend on the transaction, property, and circumstances involved.

Listing & Representation Documents

A seller may review an agreement establishing the relationship with a real estate broker or agent and describing the services, terms, responsibilities, and other conditions of the listing relationship.

Property Information & Disclosures

  • Seller property disclosures or other required disclosures
  • Property information and listing details
  • HOA or community information, when applicable
  • Existing surveys or other property documents, when applicable

Purchase Contract & Related Documents

After receiving an offer, the seller may review a purchase contract and related addenda or amendments. These documents can address price, financing, deadlines, property condition, included items, and other transaction-specific terms.

Inspection & Repair Documents

  • Inspection reports
  • Repair requests
  • Repair agreements or amendments, when applicable
  • Other property-condition documentation

Closing Documents

Before and at closing, sellers may receive documents from the title company, lender, broker, or other parties involved in the transaction. Review important documents carefully and keep copies for your records.

Quick Tip: Keep your property documents organized before listing your home. Having important records available can make it easier to respond to questions and requests during the transaction.

Tenant Forms & Documents

Tenants may encounter several forms and documents before, during, and after renting a property. The specific documents involved will depend on the property, landlord, property manager, lease terms, and other circumstances.

Rental Application & Screening Documents

Before being approved for a rental property, a prospective tenant may be asked to complete an application and provide information or documentation requested as part of the screening process.

  • Rental application
  • Identity or income documentation, when requested
  • Rental history information
  • Authorization forms related to screening, when applicable

Lease & Related Documents

If approved, the tenant may receive a lease agreement along with addenda, notices, rules, or other documents related to the tenancy. These documents may establish important responsibilities, deadlines, payment requirements, property rules, and other terms.

Move-In Documents

  • Property condition or inventory documentation, when applicable
  • Utility or service instructions
  • Property rules or community information
  • Maintenance and emergency contact instructions
  • Proof of required insurance, when applicable

During the Tenancy

  • Maintenance requests and related communications
  • Lease amendments or renewal documents
  • Notices and other written communications
  • Payment confirmations and receipts

Move-Out Documents

At the end of a tenancy, tenants may receive or provide notices, move-out instructions, property-condition documentation, forwarding information, or other documents related to the end of the lease.

Quick Tip: Keep copies of your lease, addenda, notices, payment records, and important written communications throughout your tenancy.

Disclosures, Notices & Addenda

In addition to primary contracts or lease agreements, a transaction may involve disclosures, notices, addenda, amendments, and other supporting documents. These documents can provide information, modify terms, establish deadlines, or address specific circumstances.

Disclosures

A disclosure generally provides information about a property, condition, circumstance, relationship, or other matter that may be relevant to a transaction or tenancy.

Notices

A notice may communicate important information, deadlines, rights, responsibilities, or changes related to a transaction, property, or tenancy.

Addenda & Amendments

Addenda and amendments may add to, modify, or clarify the terms of an existing agreement. The effect of a document can depend on its language and how it relates to the other documents involved.

Other Supporting Documents

  • Property information and condition documents
  • Inspection-related documents
  • HOA or community documents, when applicable
  • Financing or lender-related documents, when applicable
  • Move-in or move-out documentation, when applicable
  • Transaction-specific forms and written communications

Keep Documents Together

Because multiple documents may relate to one another, keep copies together and make sure you understand which document applies to a particular issue or stage of your transaction.

Important: Do not assume that a new document is unimportant simply because it is labeled as a notice, disclosure, addendum, or amendment. Review the document carefully and ask questions before signing when appropriate.

Before You Sign or Submit a Document

Before signing or submitting an important real estate or rental document, take time to review the information carefully. Documents may create obligations, establish deadlines, provide notices, or affect other parts of your transaction or tenancy.

Verify Important Information

  • Confirm names are spelled correctly.
  • Verify the property address.
  • Check important dates and deadlines.
  • Review financial amounts and payment information.
  • Confirm that the document is the version you intend to sign.

Review Before Signing

  • Read the document before signing or initialing.
  • Review any attached addenda or related documents.
  • Do not leave important blanks unresolved unless instructed otherwise.
  • Ask questions about information you do not understand.
  • Understand whether the document creates a deadline or obligation.

After Signing

  • Keep a copy of the completed document.
  • Save related emails or written communications.
  • Record important deadlines created by the document.
  • Keep related documents organized together.
Quick Tip: Before signing, slow down and perform one final review of the names, property address, dates, and important financial terms.

How to Keep Track of Forms & Deadlines

Real estate and rental transactions can involve multiple documents, signatures, requests, and deadlines. Keeping everything organized can help you understand what has been completed and what still requires attention.

Create a Document System

  • Keep signed documents in one secure location.
  • Save related documents together by property or transaction.
  • Use clear file names that identify the document and date.
  • Keep important emails and written communications with related records.

Track Important Deadlines

  • Record deadlines as soon as they are established.
  • Note whether action, payment, delivery, or a response is required.
  • Set reminders before important deadlines.
  • Review your transaction or lease documents when confirming dates.

Confirm What Has Been Completed

Keep track of documents that have been reviewed, signed, submitted, received, or completed. If you are unsure whether something has been received or finalized, confirm with the appropriate party.

Keep the Final Copies

Once a document has been fully executed or finalized, save a complete copy for your records. Make sure you keep the final version rather than relying only on an earlier draft.

Quick Tip: A simple checklist with document names, deadlines, and completion status can help you quickly see what still needs attention.

When You Receive a Document You Weren't Expecting

During a real estate transaction or tenancy, you may receive a form, notice, disclosure, amendment, or other document that you were not expecting. Do not assume that a document is routine or unimportant simply because it is presented for review or signature.

Pause and Review

  • Read the document before signing or submitting it.
  • Identify who sent the document and why it was provided.
  • Check whether the document creates a new obligation or deadline.
  • Review any related documents referenced within it.

Ask Questions

If you do not understand the purpose of a document or how it relates to your transaction or tenancy, ask the appropriate party for an explanation before taking action.

Verify the Source

Be especially cautious if the document involves payment instructions, sensitive personal information, or an unexpected request to sign or provide information. Verify the sender using a trusted contact method when something appears unusual.

Keep a Copy

Save a copy of important documents and related communications, including the final signed version when applicable.

Quick Tip: If a document seems unexpected, urgent, or inconsistent with what you previously discussed, pause and verify its purpose before signing or providing sensitive information.
