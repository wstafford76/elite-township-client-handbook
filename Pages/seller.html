Step 3: Pricing Your Home

Pricing your home correctly is one of the most important decisions we'll make before putting your property on the market. The goal is to establish a price that reflects current market conditions while positioning your home to attract qualified buyers.

We'll review comparable properties, recent sales, current competition, your home's condition, location, features, and other relevant market factors when developing your pricing strategy.

Factors That Influence Price

✓
Recent comparable sales
✓
Current competing listings
✓
Location and neighborhood
✓
Property size and layout
✓
Condition and updates
✓
Market supply and buyer demand
✓
Recent market trends

Pricing Strategy

The highest possible asking price is not always the best strategy. An effective listing price should balance your goals with what current market conditions indicate buyers are likely to pay.

Agent Tip: A strong pricing strategy is based on market data—not simply what a homeowner originally paid for the property or how much they have invested in it.

Step 6: Under Contract & Buyer Due Diligence

Once you and the buyer have agreed to a contract, the transaction moves into the under-contract period. During this stage, the buyer may complete inspections, secure financing, and satisfy other requirements outlined in the contract.

Your responsibilities during this period will depend on the terms of your contract. We'll keep track of important deadlines and communicate with the other parties involved in the transaction.

What May Happen During This Stage

✓
Buyer schedules a property inspection.
✓
Buyer continues the mortgage approval process.
✓
Appraisal may be completed.
✓
Additional inspections or evaluations may occur.
✓
Contract deadlines are monitored.
✓
Negotiations may occur if permitted by the contract.

Keeping the Transaction Moving

Continue maintaining the property in substantially the condition required by your contract. Keep utilities connected when necessary for inspections, appraisal, and final walk-throughs, and notify me promptly if circumstances affecting the property change.

Agent Tip: Once you're under contract, avoid making major changes to the property or removing items that are included in the transaction without first discussing them with me.

Step 7: Appraisal, Repairs & Final Negotiations

As the transaction progresses, the buyer's lender may require an appraisal and the buyer may raise questions or requests based on inspections or other contract provisions.

If an issue requires attention, we'll review the situation together and determine what response makes sense under the terms of your contract and your overall selling goals.

Appraisal

An appraisal provides an independent opinion of the property's value. When financing is involved, the buyer's lender generally uses the appraisal as part of the loan process.

Inspection & Repair Requests

Depending on the contract and circumstances, a buyer may request repairs, credits, or other concessions. A request does not necessarily mean you must agree to every item. We'll review the request and discuss your available options.

Final Negotiations

If additional negotiations are necessary, I'll help communicate the available options and document any agreed changes according to the transaction requirements.

Agent Tip: Don't agree to repairs, credits, or other changes on your own. Let me know as soon as an issue comes up so we can review the contract and determine the appropriate next step.

Step 8: Preparing for Closing

As the transaction approaches closing, we'll focus on completing the remaining contract requirements and making sure the property is ready for the final stages of the sale.

The title company, lender, buyer, and other parties will be working toward completing the transaction. I'll help keep you informed about important deadlines and items that require your attention.

Before Closing

✓
Complete any agreed-upon repairs or obligations.
✓
Keep utilities connected as required for the transaction.
✓
Remove personal belongings that are not included in the sale.
✓
Leave items included in the contract at the property.
✓
Maintain the property's condition through closing.
✓
Confirm your closing date, time, and location.
✓
Provide any requested documents to the appropriate parties.

Final Walk-Through

The buyer will typically have an opportunity to conduct a final walk-through before closing. The purpose is generally to verify that the property is in the expected condition and that agreed-upon items and repairs have been addressed.

Agent Tip: Don't remove fixtures, appliances, or other items that are included in the transaction. If you're unsure whether something stays with the property, ask before removing it.

Step 9: Closing & Transfer of Ownership

Closing is the final stage of the selling process. During closing, the required documents are signed, funds are handled according to the transaction, and ownership of the property is transferred to the buyer according to the terms of the contract.

Your title company or closing agent will coordinate the closing process and provide instructions about the documents, identification, and other items you may need to complete the transaction.

Before Closing

✓
Confirm your closing appointment.
✓
Review any documents provided for your review.
✓
Complete any remaining contractual obligations.
✓
Confirm where and how closing will take place.
✓
Follow the title company's instructions regarding identification and funds.

At Closing

You'll sign the documents required to complete the sale. Once the transaction has been properly funded and completed according to the applicable closing procedures, ownership transfers to the buyer.

After Closing

Keep copies of your final closing documents and records for your files. You should also retain documentation related to the sale for your tax and financial records and consult your appropriate tax professional regarding any tax questions.

Agent Tip: Never rely solely on emailed wiring instructions. If funds need to be wired, independently verify the instructions with the title or closing company using a trusted phone number.

Seller Frequently Asked Questions

How do you determine the listing price?

We'll review comparable sales, current competition, property condition, location, features, and current market conditions when developing your pricing strategy.

How long will it take to sell my home?

There is no guaranteed timeframe. Market conditions, pricing, property condition, location, buyer demand, and other factors can affect how quickly a property sells.

Do I have to accept the highest offer?

Not necessarily. Offers should be evaluated based on their complete terms, including price, financing, contingencies, requested concessions, deadlines, and other conditions.

What happens if the buyer requests repairs?

Depending on the contract and circumstances, a buyer may request repairs, credits, or other changes. We'll review the request and discuss your available options before you respond.

Can I continue living in the home while it is listed?

Yes, many sellers continue living in their homes while the property is listed. We'll discuss showing procedures and ways to make the process as manageable as possible.

What happens if my home doesn't sell?

If your property remains on the market, we'll review the available market feedback and discuss whether changes to pricing, presentation, marketing, or strategy may be appropriate.

When do I receive my sale proceeds?

The timing and method for receiving proceeds depend on the closing process and the title or closing company's procedures. Follow the specific instructions provided by the closing company.

Have another question? Every transaction is different. If you're unsure about something during the selling process, ask before making a decision. I'm here to help you understand your options.

✓

✓

✓

Other costs identified during the transaction

Estimated vs. Final Proceeds

An estimated seller net sheet can help you understand the potential proceeds from a sale before closing. The final amount may change as actual costs, payoff figures, prorations, and other transaction details are finalized.

Agent Tip: Don't make financial decisions based solely on the listing price. Before making important decisions, review your estimated proceeds and confirm final figures with the appropriate professionals.

Seller Timeline

Every transaction is different, but the following timeline provides a general overview of the major stages involved in selling a home.

  1. Initial Consultation
    Discuss your goals, property, timeline, and selling strategy.
  2. Property Preparation
    Complete appropriate cleaning, repairs, organization, and presentation improvements.

✓

✓

✓
Divorce or estate-related ownership issues
✓
Unresolved probate matters
✓

Wire Fraud Protection

Real estate transactions can involve large financial transfers, making wire fraud an important risk to understand. Criminals may attempt to impersonate a real estate agent, title company, lender, or other party involved in the transaction.

Protect Yourself

✓
Never rely solely on emailed wiring instructions.
✓
Do not click unexpected links requesting financial information.
✓
Be cautious of last-minute changes to wiring instructions.
✓
Independently verify wiring instructions before sending funds.
✓
Use a trusted phone number to verify instructions with the title or closing company.
✓
Never send account information in response to an unexpected request.

If Something Looks Suspicious

Stop and independently verify the request before taking action. If you believe fraudulent wiring instructions may have been sent, contact the appropriate financial institution and closing professionals immediately.

Important: Treat any last-minute request to change payment or wiring instructions as suspicious until independently verified. When in doubt, stop and verify using a trusted phone number.
