Step 2: Prepare Your Property

Before your property is marketed for rent, we'll evaluate its condition, presentation, and readiness for prospective tenants. The goal is to present the property in a clean, functional, and well-maintained condition while addressing items that may affect marketability or tenant interest.

We'll discuss which maintenance, repairs, cleaning, or improvements make sense based on the property's condition, your timeline, budget, and overall leasing strategy.

Areas to Consider

✓
Addressing necessary maintenance and repair items.
✓
Deep cleaning the property before marketing and showings.
✓
Checking major systems and appliances for proper operation.
✓
Improving curb appeal and exterior presentation.
✓
Removing unnecessary personal belongings and clutter.
✓
Making sure the property is safe and accessible for showings.
✓
Considering appropriate improvements that may increase marketability.

Before Making Major Improvements

Not every improvement will provide the same benefit for a rental property. Before spending significant money on renovations or upgrades, we'll consider whether the improvement is likely to support your rental strategy, attract qualified applicants, or improve the property's marketability.

Agent Tip: The goal is not necessarily to make the property perfect or to complete every possible upgrade. The focus should be on presenting a clean, functional, well-maintained property that is ready for qualified tenants.

Step 3: Determine Your Rental Strategy

Establishing the right rental strategy is an important part of preparing your property for the market. We'll review current rental market conditions and comparable properties to help determine a strategy that supports your goals while positioning the property competitively.

Rental pricing is influenced by a variety of factors, including the property's location, condition, size, features, current competition, and overall supply and demand in the rental market.

Factors We'll Consider

✓
Recent and current comparable rental properties
✓
Current competing rental listings
✓
Property location and neighborhood
✓
Property size, layout, and features
✓
Property condition and recent improvements
✓
Current rental market supply and tenant demand
✓
Seasonal and recent rental market trends

Setting a Rental Price

The highest possible advertised rent is not always the most effective strategy. A rental price that is significantly above comparable properties may limit interest and increase the amount of time the property remains vacant.

We'll use available market information to develop a rental strategy that considers your financial goals, the property's characteristics, and current market conditions.

Agent Tip: Rental pricing should be based on current market data and the property's competitive position. The goal is to attract qualified applicants while supporting a strategy that makes sense for your overall objectives.

Step 5: Review Applications & Select a Tenant

Once prospective tenants begin applying, the application and screening process helps determine whether an applicant meets the landlord's established rental requirements. Requirements, screening procedures, and final approval decisions should be handled consistently and in accordance with applicable laws and policies.

Depending on the property and the established requirements, applications may include information related to income, rental history, employment, credit, background screening, and other information requested through the approved application process.

Application Information May Include

✓
Personal identification and contact information
✓
Employment and income information
✓
Current and previous rental history
✓
Information about other proposed occupants, when required
✓
Credit, background, or other screening information as permitted and applicable
✓
Additional documentation required by the established application process

Establishing Rental Criteria

Before reviewing applications, it is important to have clear rental criteria and a consistent process for evaluating applicants. This may include minimum income requirements, rental history standards, credit criteria, occupancy requirements, pet policies, and other property- specific requirements.

Application requirements and tenant selection decisions should be handled consistently and in accordance with applicable fair housing laws and other legal requirements.

Reviewing Applications

Applications and supporting information can then be reviewed according to the established requirements. If additional documentation or clarification is needed, the appropriate request can be made through the established application process.

Agent Tip: Establish your rental criteria before applications are reviewed and apply those criteria consistently. Clear requirements and a documented process can help create a more organized and professional leasing experience.

Agent Tip: A well-prepared move-in can help prevent confusion later. Make sure the property is ready, required documents are organized, and the tenant understands the appropriate process for important issues such as maintenance, property access, and applicable rules.

Step 9: Managing the Lease & Preparing for Renewal or Move-Out

After the tenant moves in, the focus shifts to managing the tenancy in accordance with the lease, maintaining the property, addressing appropriate concerns, and preparing for the eventual renewal or end of the lease term.

Good communication, organized records, and clear procedures can help make the tenancy easier to manage and help prepare both parties for renewal or move-out when the lease term approaches its end.

During the Tenancy

✓
Maintain records related to the lease and tenancy.
✓
Address maintenance and repair responsibilities as required.
✓
Keep records of significant maintenance, repairs, and property issues.
✓
Communicate important property or tenancy information when appropriate.
✓
Follow the applicable lease terms and established procedures.
✓
Keep track of important lease dates, deadlines, and notice requirements.

As the Lease Approaches Expiration

✓
Review the lease expiration date and applicable notice requirements.
✓
Consider whether renewing the lease may be appropriate.
✓
Review current rental market conditions.
✓
Evaluate the property's condition and any anticipated maintenance needs.
✓
Communicate renewal or move-out expectations according to the applicable lease and requirements.

If the Tenant Is Moving Out

When a tenancy is ending, preparation can help make the turnover process more organized. Review the lease and applicable move-out procedures so expectations regarding possession, property condition, keys, access devices, and other responsibilities are clear.

✓
Review the tenant's move-out date and any required notices.
✓
Review applicable move-out responsibilities and property requirements.
✓
Coordinate the return of keys, remotes, and other access devices.
✓
Document the property's condition after possession is returned.
✓
Identify maintenance, repairs, or cleaning needed before the next tenancy.
✓
Organize records and documentation related to the completed tenancy.
Agent Tip: Staying organized throughout the tenancy makes the renewal or move-out process much easier. Keep important records, track key lease dates, and begin planning before the lease expiration date approaches.

Landlord Frequently Asked Questions

The following questions address common topics that may arise when preparing, marketing, and leasing a rental property. Specific requirements may vary depending on the property, lease, applicable laws, and other circumstances.

How is the rental price determined?

Rental pricing is generally based on factors such as the property's location, size, condition, features, current market conditions, and comparable rental properties. The overall leasing strategy may also take into account your preferred timeline and other property-specific considerations.

How long will it take to find a tenant?

The amount of time a property remains available can vary depending on rental price, property condition, location, demand, competition, and current market conditions. Preparing and pricing the property appropriately can help improve its ability to attract prospective tenants.

What information is typically needed from a prospective tenant?

Application requirements may include identification, contact information, employment and income information, rental history, and other information required through the established application and screening process.

How are tenant applications reviewed?

Applications should be reviewed according to the landlord's established rental criteria and applicable requirements. Depending on the application process, additional documentation or clarification may be requested before a final decision is made.

Who makes the final decision about selecting a tenant?

The landlord is generally responsible for making the final decision regarding an applicant, subject to applicable laws, established rental criteria, and any other requirements that apply to the property or transaction.

What happens after an applicant is approved?

Once an applicant is approved, the next steps may include preparing the lease and applicable addenda, confirming required payments, completing the signing process, and coordinating the property's move-in preparations.

What should I do before the tenant moves in?

Before move-in, prepare the property for occupancy, complete applicable repairs or maintenance, organize required documents, gather keys and access devices, and make sure important property information and maintenance procedures are ready to be provided.

What happens when the lease is about to expire?

As the lease approaches expiration, review the applicable notice requirements and consider whether to renew the tenancy or prepare for the tenant's move-out. Current market conditions and the property's anticipated needs may also be considered.

What should I do when a tenant moves out?

Follow the applicable lease and move-out procedures, document the property's condition, confirm the return of keys and access devices, identify needed cleaning or repairs, and prepare the property for its next use or tenancy.

Agent Tip: Every rental property and tenancy is different. When questions arise, reviewing the applicable lease, property requirements, and established procedures can help determine the appropriate next step.
