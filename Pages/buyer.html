Step 2: Get Pre-Approved

Before shopping for a home, it's important to know how much you can comfortably afford. A mortgage pre-approval gives you a realistic budget and shows sellers that you're a serious buyer.

During pre-approval, your lender will review your income, assets, debts, credit history, and employment to determine the loan amount you may qualify for.

Benefits of Getting Pre-Approved

✓
Know your price range before searching.
✓
Strengthen your offer when competing with other buyers.
✓
Identify potential financing issues early.
✓
Move more quickly when you find the right home.
Agent Tip: Shop for a home based on a monthly payment you're comfortable with, not just the maximum amount a lender approves.

Understanding Your Monthly Home Payment

When deciding how much home you can comfortably afford, it's important to look beyond the purchase price. Your total monthly housing cost may include several different expenses.

Common Parts of a Monthly Housing Payment

  • Principal: The portion of your payment that goes toward repaying the amount borrowed.
  • Interest: The cost charged by the lender for borrowing money.
  • Property Taxes: Taxes associated with the property, which may be included in your monthly mortgage payment depending on your loan and escrow arrangement.
  • Homeowners Insurance: Insurance coverage required by most lenders to help protect the property.
  • Mortgage Insurance: May apply depending on your loan program, down payment, and other qualifying factors.
  • HOA or Condo Fees: If applicable, these are separate recurring costs that should be included in your monthly budget.

Don't Forget About Other Homeownership Expenses

Your mortgage payment is only part of the cost of owning a home. You should also consider utilities, routine maintenance, repairs, furnishings, and other expenses that may come with the property.

Agent Tip: Instead of focusing only on the maximum loan amount you may qualify for, consider the total monthly cost of the home and choose a payment that fits comfortably within your overall budget.

Choosing & Comparing Homes

After viewing several properties, it can become difficult to remember which features, concerns, and advantages belonged to each home. Comparing homes using the same criteria can help you make a more organized and confident decision.

Focus on Your Priorities

Before beginning your search, identify which features are must-haves, which features are preferred, and which items you may be willing to compromise on.

  • Location and commute
  • Monthly housing cost
  • Home size and layout
  • Condition and age of major systems
  • Lot size and outdoor space
  • Neighborhood and nearby amenities
  • Potential future maintenance or improvements

Compare the Whole Picture

A lower purchase price does not always mean a home will cost less to own. Consider the property's condition, estimated maintenance needs, taxes, insurance, HOA fees when applicable, and any improvements you may want or need to make after closing.

Agent Tip: Try not to judge a home based on one feature alone. A home with the perfect kitchen may not be the best overall fit if the location, monthly cost, condition, or other important factors do not match your priorities.

New Construction Considerations

Buying a newly constructed home can be different from purchasing an existing property. The home may still be under construction, the builder may have its own contract and timelines, and some decisions may need to be made before the home is complete.

Things to Consider

  • The builder's contract and any applicable addenda.
  • Construction timelines and the possibility of delays.
  • Included features versus upgrades that may increase the final cost.
  • Lot selection, community rules, and HOA requirements when applicable.
  • Builder warranties and what they cover.
  • The timing and requirements for financing approval.
  • Independent inspections during construction or before closing, when appropriate.

Representation Matters

When visiting a new construction community, ask about the builder's policies regarding outside real estate representation. If you are working with me, let the builder's sales representative know before registering or beginning the purchase process.

Agent Tip: A model home can show upgraded finishes and features that may not be included in the base price. Before making a decision, review exactly what is included, what costs extra, and how any upgrades may affect your overall budget.

Step 4: Making an Offer

Once you've found the right home, we'll prepare and submit a written offer to the seller. An offer is more than just the purchase price—it also includes important terms, deadlines, and conditions that protect your interests.

I'll explain each part of the offer before you sign anything and help you develop a strategy based on current market conditions.

Your Offer May Include

✓
Purchase price
✓
Earnest money amount
✓
Option period (if applicable)
✓
Requested closing date
✓
Seller concessions or credits
✓
Financing and appraisal contingencies, if applicable

How We Build Your Offer

Before submitting an offer, we'll review the property, current market conditions, and the terms that are most important to you. The purchase price is only one part of the overall offer.

We'll discuss the available information about the property and consider factors such as recent comparable sales, competing buyer activity when known, the property's time on the market, and the seller's stated preferences or timeline when available.

Important Terms to Consider

✓
The purchase price you are comfortable offering.
✓
The amount of earnest money, if applicable.
✓
The option period and option fee, if applicable.
✓
Your requested closing date.
✓
Financing terms and applicable contract provisions.
✓
Seller concessions or contributions you may request.
✓
Other terms that may be important to your situation.

What Happens After We Submit the Offer?

After your offer is submitted, the seller may accept the offer, reject it, make a counteroffer, or allow the offer to expire according to the terms and deadlines involved.

✓
Accepted: The seller agrees to the offer, and once the contract is fully executed, the transaction moves into the next stage.
✓
Countered: The seller proposes changes to one or more terms. You can review the proposed changes and decide how you would like to respond.
✓
Rejected: The seller chooses not to accept the offer. We can discuss other available properties or your next steps.

I'll help you review any response you receive and explain the available options so you can make an informed decision.

Agent Tip: The strongest offer isn't always the highest price. Flexible terms, a solid pre-approval, and realistic timelines can also make your offer more attractive to a seller.

Understanding the Texas Option Period

Depending on the terms negotiated in your contract, your purchase may include an option period. This is an important period of time during which you may have certain contractual rights to further evaluate the property and decide whether to continue with the purchase.

The specific length of the option period, any option fee, and the deadlines that apply are determined by the terms of your executed contract. It is important to understand those deadlines and act promptly if additional inspections, evaluations, or decisions are needed.

What You May Want to Do During This Time

✓
Schedule and complete a professional home inspection.
✓
Review the inspection report and other available property information.
✓
Consider whether additional specialist evaluations are needed.
✓
Discuss significant concerns or findings with the appropriate professionals.
✓
Review your available options before important contract deadlines expire.

Why Timing Matters

The option period and other contract deadlines can move quickly. Scheduling inspections early gives you more time to review findings, ask questions, and make informed decisions before applicable deadlines.

Important: Your rights and obligations are controlled by your specific contract. If you are unsure about a deadline, notice, or contractual option, contact me as soon as possible so we can review the situation and determine the appropriate next steps.

One of the most important parts of buying a home is the professional home inspection. The inspection helps identify the condition of the property and may reveal issues that aren't visible during a showing.

A licensed home inspector will evaluate major systems and components of the home. After the inspection, you'll receive a written report that we'll review together.

Items Commonly Inspected

✓
Foundation and structural components
✓
Heating and air conditioning (HVAC)
✓
Electrical system
✓
Plumbing system
✓
Windows and doors
✓
Appliances included with the home

After the Inspection

Based on the inspection results, you may decide to request repairs, negotiate a credit, proceed with the purchase as-is, or, depending on the terms of your contract, exercise your contractual options.

Reviewing Inspection Findings

Inspection reports can be detailed and may identify a wide range of observations, from routine maintenance items to more significant concerns. Not every item in an inspection report requires the same level of attention.

We'll review the findings together and help organize your questions and concerns so you can focus on the items that are most important to your decision about moving forward.

Prioritizing Your Concerns

✓
Health or safety concerns.
✓
Structural or foundation-related issues.
✓
Roof, plumbing, electrical, or HVAC concerns.
✓
Items that may require evaluation by a qualified specialist.
✓
Major repairs or conditions that could result in significant expense.
✓
Routine maintenance or cosmetic items that you may choose to address after closing.

Repair Requests & Negotiations

Depending on the inspection findings, your contract, and the applicable deadlines, you may decide to move forward without requesting changes, request repairs or other modifications, seek a credit or other negotiated solution, or consider other options available under your contract.

The seller is not automatically required to agree to every request. Negotiations may involve discussion and compromise, and the final outcome will depend on the circumstances and the terms agreed to by the parties.

Agent Tip: Focus first on significant concerns rather than creating a long list of minor cosmetic or routine maintenance items. The goal is to make an informed decision and address the issues that matter most to you.

Step 7: Appraisal & Financing

After the inspection, your lender will continue processing your loan and, if required, order an appraisal. The appraisal is performed by an independent licensed appraiser to estimate the home's market value.

The lender uses the appraisal to help ensure the property's value supports the loan amount. During this time, your lender may also ask for updated financial documents or additional information.

What to Expect

✓
The lender orders the appraisal.
✓
The appraiser visits the property.
✓
The appraisal report is completed.
✓
Your lender reviews the report.
✓
Your loan continues toward final approval.

If the Appraisal Comes In Low

A low appraisal doesn't automatically end the transaction. Depending on the contract and the circumstances, there may be options to negotiate, provide additional information, or adjust the terms of the transaction. We'll review your options together if this situation arises.

Avoiding Delays Before Closing

Your lender may continue reviewing your financial information throughout the loan process. Changes to your finances, employment, or credit can affect your loan approval or create additional documentation requirements.

Until Closing, Avoid Making Major Changes Unless You Have Discussed Them With Your Lender

✓
Opening new credit cards or financing new purchases.
✓
Making large purchases that could affect your available funds or debt.
✓
Changing jobs or employment status.
✓
Moving money between accounts without keeping clear documentation.
✓
Making large deposits that cannot be easily documented.
✓
Closing existing credit accounts.

If a financial change is necessary, contact your lender before taking action whenever possible. They can explain whether the change may affect your loan and what documentation may be required.

Agent Tip: Until your transaction has closed, keep your financial situation as stable as possible and continue responding promptly to requests from your lender. A quick response can help prevent unnecessary delays.

Step 8: Clear to Close & Closing Day

Once your lender has approved your loan and all contract requirements have been satisfied, you'll receive a "Clear to Close." This means your lender is ready to fund the loan and you're in the final stage of the home buying process.

Before closing, you'll receive a Closing Disclosure that outlines your final loan terms and the amount you'll need to bring to closing. Review this document carefully and ask questions if anything is unclear.

Before Closing Day

✓
Review your Closing Disclosure.
✓
Schedule your final walk-through of the property.
✓
Arrange a wire transfer or cashier's check if instructed.
✓
Bring a valid government-issued photo ID.
✓
Confirm the time and location of your closing appointment.

Your Final Walk-Through

The final walk-through is typically your opportunity to confirm the property's condition before closing and verify that the home is in the condition required by your contract.

What to Check

✓
Confirm that agreed-upon repairs or other negotiated items have been completed, if applicable.
✓
Check that included fixtures, appliances, and other items remain at the property as required by the contract.
✓
Look for any new damage or significant changes since your previous visit.
✓
Test major systems or features when appropriate.
✓
Make sure personal property that was not agreed to remain has been removed.
✓
Bring any concerns to my attention before closing so they can be addressed appropriately.
Agent Tip: The final walk-through is not another home inspection. Its purpose is to confirm the property's condition and identify any issues that may need to be addressed before closing.

What Happens at Closing?

You'll sign the final loan and purchase documents, complete any remaining payments, and once the transaction is funded and recorded, you'll officially become the owner of your new home.

Agent Tip: Always verify wire instructions directly with the title company using a trusted phone number. Never rely solely on emailed wiring instructions, as real estate transactions are common targets for wire fraud.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to buy a home?

Every transaction is different, but many home purchases are completed within 30 to 45 days after a contract is accepted.

Do I need a 20% down payment?

No. Many loan programs allow qualified buyers to purchase a home with a much smaller down payment. Your lender can explain the options that best fit your financial situation.

What is earnest money?

Earnest money is a deposit made after your offer is accepted. It shows the seller you're serious about purchasing the home and is generally applied toward your purchase at closing according to the contract.

Can I back out after my offer is accepted?

Your rights depend on the terms of your purchase contract and applicable deadlines. Contact me immediately if you're considering making changes after the contract has been signed.

When do I get the keys?

In most transactions, you'll receive the keys after the sale has funded and the deed has been recorded, unless your contract provides otherwise.

Texas Home Buying Tips

Buying a home in Texas has some unique practices and timelines. Knowing what to expect can help you feel more confident throughout the transaction.

Option Period

Many Texas contracts include an option period. During this time, you typically have the opportunity to inspect the property and decide whether to continue with the purchase under the terms of your contract.

Earnest Money

Earnest money is typically delivered to the title company after the contract is fully executed. The contract specifies when it must be delivered.

Title Company

In Texas, a title company commonly coordinates the closing process, handles escrow, prepares many closing documents, and facilitates the transfer of funds according to the contract.

Property Taxes

Texas does not have a state income tax, but property taxes can be a significant part of homeownership. Be sure to review estimated property taxes as part of your overall housing budget.

Agent Tip: If you have questions about any contract deadline or document, ask before signing. Understanding each step helps you make informed decisions.

Who's Who in Your Transaction

Buying a home involves several professionals working together to help complete your purchase. Understanding each person's role can make the process much easier to follow.

Your Real Estate Agent

I help you find homes, explain the buying process, prepare offers, negotiate terms, coordinate deadlines, and guide you from our first meeting through closing.

Your Lender

Your lender evaluates your finances, approves your mortgage, prepares your loan documents, and provides the funds needed to purchase your home.

The Title Company

The title company researches the property's ownership history, helps coordinate the closing, manages escrow funds, and facilitates the transfer of ownership according to the contract.

The Home Inspector

A licensed home inspector evaluates the property's condition and prepares a report identifying the home's major systems, components, and any visible concerns.

The Appraiser

The appraiser is selected by the lender to provide an independent opinion of the home's market value.

Your Insurance Agent

Before closing, you'll obtain homeowners insurance. Your insurance agent will help you choose a policy that meets your lender's requirements and protects your investment.

Remember: Although many professionals are involved, I'll help coordinate the process and keep you informed every step of the way.

Texas Homestead Exemption

If the home you purchase will be your principal residence, you may be eligible for a Texas residence homestead exemption. A qualifying homestead exemption can reduce the portion of your home's value that is subject to certain property taxes.

After You Become a Homeowner

✓
Confirm whether you qualify for a residence homestead exemption.
✓
Contact the appraisal district for the county where the property is located.
✓
Obtain the current residence homestead exemption application and instructions.
✓
Submit your application and any required documentation by the applicable deadline.
✓
Keep a copy of your application and supporting documents for your records.
Important: Homestead eligibility, exemptions, deadlines, and local requirements can vary. Your local appraisal district is the appropriate source for determining your eligibility and processing your application.
